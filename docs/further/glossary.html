Glossary

The terms of survival analysis used in this tutorial, with the page where each is explained.
Adjusted survival curve
The survival curve each group would have if all groups had the same distribution of the other variables of a Cox model; the module computes it by direct standardisation. Adjusted curves
At risk
A patient is at risk at time \(t\) if they are alive (event-free) and still under observation just before \(t\). The number at risk \(n_j\) is the denominator of each Kaplan-Meier step. Kaplan-Meier
Baseline hazard, \(h_0(t)\)
The hazard of a patient with all variables at their reference level (or 0) in a Cox model. Its shape is not estimated as part of the hazard ratios. Cox
C-index (concordance)
The probability that, of two patients whose order of events is known, the one the model predicts to be at higher risk has the event first. 0.5 = chance, 1 = perfect. Cox
Censoring (right censoring)
Observation of a patient ends before the event: their survival time is known to be longer than the observed time. Administrative censoring happens when the study closes; informative censoring, when the reason for censoring is related to prognosis, biases the estimates. Censoring
Competing risk
An event that prevents the event of interest (death from another cause when the event is death from cancer). Treating it as censoring overestimates the cumulative incidence. Kaplan-Meier
Confounding
A distortion of the association between a variable and the outcome because of a third variable associated with both. In the tutorial, part of the crude effect of age was due to ECOG. Cox example
Cox regression (proportional hazards model)
\(h(t \mid x) = h_0(t)\exp(\beta_1 x_1 + \dots)\): a regression model for the hazard, which gives hazard ratios adjusted for the other variables. Cox
Cumulative hazard, \(H(t)\)
The hazard added up from 0 to \(t\); \(S(t) = e^{-H(t)}\). Time-to-event data
Cumulative incidence
The probability of having had the event by time \(t\), \(1 - S(t)\) when there are no competing risks.
Direct standardisation
Averaging the predicted survival curves of all patients after setting the variable of interest to the same level for everyone; used for adjusted survival curves.
Effect modification (interaction)
The effect of one variable depends on the value of another. Tested with an interaction term in a Cox model. Interactions
Event
What ends the survival time: death, relapse, progression. Its definition, and the time origin, must be stated.
Events per variable
The number of events divided by the number of coefficients of a model; roughly 10 or more is a common rule of thumb. Cox
Greenwood’s formula
The variance of the Kaplan-Meier estimate, used for its confidence interval. Kaplan-Meier
Hazard, \(h(t)\)
The rate of the event at time \(t\) among patients still event-free just before \(t\); a rate, with units (per month), not a probability. Time-to-event data
Hazard ratio (HR)
The ratio of the hazards of two groups (or per unit of a covariate). HR = 1: no difference; HR < 1: lower hazard. It is not a risk ratio. Cox
Kaplan-Meier estimator
The product of the conditional probabilities of surviving each event time, \(\prod (1 - d_j/n_j)\); the standard estimate of the survival curve. Kaplan-Meier
Log-log plot
\(\log(-\log \widehat S(t))\) against log time, by group; parallel curves support proportional hazards. Assumptions
Log-rank test
Compares survival curves by adding up observed minus expected events at each event time. Weighted versions: Gehan-Breslow, Tarone-Ware, Peto-Peto. Log-rank
Median follow-up
The median time patients were (or would have been) followed, estimated with the reverse Kaplan-Meier method: the event and censoring indicators are swapped. KM example
Median survival
The first time at which the Kaplan-Meier curve reaches 0.5 or less. “Not reached” if fewer than half of the patients had the event. Kaplan-Meier
Partial likelihood
The likelihood that the Cox model maximises: at each event time, the probability that the patient who died was the one to die among those at risk. The baseline hazard cancels out.
Proportional hazards
The assumption of the Cox model that each hazard ratio is constant over time. Assumptions
Reference level
The level of a factor that the others are compared with (HR = 1); in jamovi, the first level of the variable.
Schoenfeld residuals
For each death, the variable’s value in the patient who died minus its average in the risk set; plotted against time, they show whether a coefficient changes over time. Assumptions
Stratified Cox model
A Cox model with a separate baseline hazard in each stratum and common hazard ratios; the stratification variable gets no HR. Assumptions
Survival function, \(S(t)\)
The probability of being event-free beyond time \(t\), \(P(T > t)\).
Trend test
A 1-df test that survival (or the hazard) changes steadily along the levels of an ordered factor. Log-rank
Univariable and multivariable
A univariable (crude) model has one variable; a multivariable model estimates each variable’s effect adjusted for the others.
Wald, likelihood ratio and score tests
Three ways of testing coefficients of a Cox model; the global versions test that all hazard ratios are 1. With one factor, the score test is the log-rank test.
